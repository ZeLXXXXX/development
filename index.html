🇵🇭 Philippine Context

Why Preparedness Matters Here

The Philippines occupies the most disaster-exposed position on Earth, situated along the Pacific Typhoon Belt, within the Ring of Fire, and exposed to three active monsoon systems. With over 110 million people at risk, community-level preparedness is the single most effective tool against typhoon deaths.

Super Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda, 2013) killed over 6,300 Filipinos — and most deaths were preventable with proper evacuation protocols. TyphoonReady PH exists to fill that education gap.

⚡ PAGASA Warning System

The Philippines uses a 1–5 Typhoon Signal system. Signal No. 1 means gusts of 30–60 km/h. Signal No. 5 (the highest) means gusts exceed 220 km/h — expect catastrophic damage.

🌊 Storm Surge Danger

Storm surge — not rain — is the leading cause of typhoon deaths in the Philippines. Coastal barangays can be inundated by walls of water 3–6 meters high within minutes of landfall.

✅ Early Evacuation Saves Lives

Evacuating 12–24 hours before landfall reduces mortality risk by over 80%. The single most important decision in a typhoon is leaving early, before roads flood and roads become inaccessible.
